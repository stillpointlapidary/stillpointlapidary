# Still Point Lapidary — Encyclopedia Approved Sources

**Status:** Draft for Christie review  
**Purpose:** Defines which sources may be used for encyclopedia research, how they are ranked, and how conflicts or thin coverage must be handled.

---

## 1. Authority and Scope

This document controls:

- approved research sources
- source tiers
- source eligibility
- consensus thresholds
- specialist-source use
- excluded sources
- conflict handling
- source-recording requirements

This document does **not** control:

- writing style
- page structure
- field counts
- database storage
- stone-specific approved conclusions
- final editorial approval

Related authorities:

- `ENCYCLOPEDIA-WRITING-AND-RESEARCH.md` controls research method and claim framing
- `ENCYCLOPEDIA-CONTENT-FIELDS.md` controls required fields
- canonical research records preserve exact citations and evidence notes
- canonical MD contains approved public copy

Use only the sources and rules in this document unless Christie or Dustin explicitly approves an exception or update.

## 2. Metaphysical Sources

### Tier A+

Use as foundational book sources when the relevant stone is covered.

- *Crystalpedia* by Athena Perrakis
- *The Crystal Bible* by Judy Hall
- *The Crystal Bible 2* by Judy Hall
- *The Crystal Bible 3* by Judy Hall
- *The Crystal Encyclopedia* by Judy Hall
- *The Book of Stones* by Robert Simmons and Naisha Ahsian

Tier A+ sources are especially useful for:

- defining metaphysical identity
- chakra
- element
- broad traditional themes
- historical crystal-practice context
- cross-checking major claims

A single A+ source does not automatically settle a conflict when stronger cross-source disagreement exists.

### Tier A

Use as the primary online consensus core.

- Crystal Vaults
- Crystal Council
- Moonrise Crystals
- Crystals.com
- Crystal Almanac

Tier A sources may establish or confirm:

- major metaphysical themes
- chakra
- element
- zodiac
- practical use cases
- secondary and occasional associations
- common modern crystal-practice interpretations

### Tier B

Use as supporting sources only.

- Energy Muse
- Healing Crystals

Tier B may:

- reinforce an established pattern
- help identify a possible secondary association
- support thin-source research when stronger sources are limited

Tier B may not:

- independently establish a major claim
- override Tier A+ or Tier A consensus
- resolve a dispute by itself
- count as the sole basis for a required field

## 3. Metaphysical Consensus Rules

### 3.1 Major Claims

A major metaphysical claim should be supported by:

- Tier A+ coverage, or
- strong agreement across at least 3 approved Tier A or A+ sources

### 3.2 Secondary Claims

A secondary claim should have meaningful agreement across at least 2 approved sources.

### 3.3 Occasional Associations

An occasional association must be:

- supported by at least one credible approved source
- consistent with the broader identity
- clearly framed as secondary or situational
- omitted when it adds little or creates confusion

### 3.4 Conflicts

When approved sources materially disagree:

1. record the disagreement
2. assess source strength and specificity
3. reduce claim strength if needed
4. omit the claim when no defensible synthesis exists
5. ask Christie or Dustin when the conflict affects a required field or locked production value

Do not silently choose the most convenient answer.

## 4. Field-Specific Metaphysical Sources

### Energetic Role

Derive from cohort-wide metaphysical consensus.

Use the strongest repeated pattern across approved sources.

Energetic Role must not be assigned merely because a role icon or catalog slot exists.

### Chakra

Use Tier A+ and Tier A consensus.

When a secondary chakra is weak or inconsistent, omit it.

### Element

Use *The Book of Stones* plus Tier A normalization.

Normalize `Wind` to `Air` in public copy and production data.

Preserve original source wording in internal notes where relevant.

### Zodiac

Use *Crystalpedia* as a primary reference where covered, with Tier A confirmation when available.

When approved sources conflict or the assignment is thin, flag rather than inflate.

### Planet

Planet is no longer researched for new encyclopedia production.

Existing stored values may be preserved, but Planet is not displayed and is not a required field.

### Color Energy

Derive from:

- the stone’s physical appearance
- approved catalog vocabulary
- editorial normalization across the cohort

Color Energy is not established by metaphysical retailers alone.

## 5. Geological and Technical Sources

### Core Sources

Use these as the primary foundation for identity, physical properties, formation, locality, and treatment research:

1. Mindat
2. GIA, when the material is covered
3. Handbook of Mineralogy

### Specialist Sources

Use these for targeted verification.

#### IMA-CNMNC

Use for:

- accepted mineral names
- species status
- nomenclature
- questionable names
- disputed mineral identity
- official classification

#### RRUFF

Use for:

- chemistry
- Raman data
- XRD
- IR
- crystallographic verification
- technical identity questions

#### USGS MRDS

Use for:

- deposits
- occurrences
- mining districts
- locality verification

#### Walter Schumann, *Gemstones of the World*

Use for:

- standard gemological context
- gem varieties
- treatment background
- common trade distinctions

### Additional Qualified Technical Sources

Museums, universities, scientific institutions, peer-reviewed papers, and official geological surveys may be used when:

- the source is directly relevant
- authorship or institutional authority is clear
- the claim falls within the source’s expertise
- the source materially improves verification

Record why the source was used.

## 6. Identity and Treatment Research

Use the strongest relevant technical source for:

- mineral versus rock classification
- composite materials
- treated quartz
- synthetics
- fossil material
- organic material
- trade-name mixtures
- disputed commercial names
- locality varieties
- coating methods
- dyeing
- irradiation
- heat treatment
- fracture filling
- reconstructed material

Retailer descriptions do not establish identity or treatment.

When treatment varies by specimen:

- do not universalize
- describe the common market pattern
- preserve provenance uncertainty
- distinguish what can and cannot be determined visually

---

## 7. Market and Collector Sources

Retailer pages may be consulted only for clearly labeled market observation, such as:

- common selling forms
- current market terminology
- broad availability
- recurring mislabeling
- specimen presentation
- price drivers that require current market context

Retailer pages may not establish:

- mineral identity
- chemical composition
- treatment status
- metaphysical consensus
- geological formation
- locality fact without independent verification

Market claims must be synthesized cautiously because availability and pricing change.

## 8. Not Approved for Routine Research

The following sources are not approved for routine encyclopedia research unless Christie or Dustin authorizes a specific use:

- Sage Goddess
- Hibiscus Moon
- New Moon Beginnings
- Tiny Rituals
- Rock Paradise

These sources:

- do not count toward research minimums
- do not establish consensus
- do not resolve conflicts
- must not remain in the counted source set

## 9. Excluded Sources

Do not use these as approved encyclopedia sources:

- Charms of Light
- Fire Mountain Gems
- Healing Hedge Witch
- unsupported retailer copy
- autogenerated summaries
- anonymous listicles
- AI-generated research summaries
- unresolved or unvetted sources
- social media posts as factual authority
- marketplace listings as identity evidence

A source may be mentioned in internal notes as an example of market confusion, but it does not become an approved research source by being recorded.

## 10. Source Minimums

### Standard Stone

Metaphysical:

- 6–10 approved sources

Technical:

- 2–4 qualified sources

### Thin-Source Stone

Metaphysical:

- 4–6 approved sources

Technical:

- use all strong relevant coverage available

Thin-source status must be recorded.

Do not pad a thin-source stone with weak or excluded sources to reach a number.

---

## 11. Source Precedence

Use this order when sources conflict within their domain.

### Metaphysical

1. strong cross-source consensus
2. Tier A+ foundational coverage
3. Tier A agreement
4. Tier B support

No single source automatically overrides broad stronger consensus.

### Geological and Technical

1. IMA-CNMNC for official nomenclature and species status
2. direct scientific or institutional evidence relevant to the claim
3. Mindat, GIA, and Handbook of Mineralogy
4. specialist sources such as RRUFF and USGS MRDS
5. reputable gemological references

Precedence depends on the question. Use the source with the most direct authority over that issue.

---

## 12. Conflict Handling

When reliable sources conflict:

1. identify the exact point of disagreement
2. distinguish terminology differences from factual disagreement
3. check publication date and scope
4. check whether one source discusses a trade name while another discusses a mineral species
5. consult the strongest specialist source
6. record the unresolved issue
7. reduce or omit the public claim when necessary
8. ask Christie or Dustin before changing a locked value

Never conceal disagreement to produce a cleaner paragraph.

---

## 13. Source Recording

Internal research records must include:

- exact source title
- author or institution
- URL or publication details where applicable
- source tier
- access date for online sources when useful
- claim or field supported
- conflicts
- rejected claims
- treatment uncertainty
- identity ambiguity
- thin-source status

Do not name sources in public copy unless editorially necessary.

---

## 14. Public Copy and Attribution

Public encyclopedia copy should read as an integrated reference entry, not a stitched literature review.

Do not use routine phrases such as:

- “According to Crystal Vaults...”
- “Judy Hall says...”
- “Mindat reports...”

Name a source publicly only when:

- the source itself is part of the historical story
- a formal classification decision matters
- attribution is necessary for accuracy
- Christie or Dustin approves it

---

## 15. Source Audit

Before research is marked complete, verify:

- required source minimums are met or thin-source status is documented
- all counted sources are approved
- major claims have appropriate support
- geological claims use qualified technical sources
- identity and treatment questions use specialist verification where needed
- source conflicts are recorded
- rejected sources are not counted
- retailer copy has not been used as factual authority
- Planet has not been researched as a required field
- public copy contains no unnecessary source names

Do not mark source review complete when an unapproved source remains in the counted evidence set.

---

## 16. Change Control

Adding, removing, or reclassifying a source requires:

1. Christie or Dustin approval
2. update to this document
3. review of active research that used the previous rule
4. review of any affected locked field or draft

This document reflects the current approved source policy.

Do not retain superseded source rules in an amendment appendix.
