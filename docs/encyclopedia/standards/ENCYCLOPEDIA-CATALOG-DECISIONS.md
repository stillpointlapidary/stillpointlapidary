## 6. Catalog Identity Policy

Every approved catalog entry may retain its own encyclopedia page whether it is:

- a mineral species
- a mineral variety
- a rock
- a mineraloid
- an organic material
- a mineral aggregate
- a composite
- a synthetic material
- a treated material
- a fossil
- a trade name
- a locality designation
- a commercial grouping

A familiar market name may remain the page title when approved.

The encyclopedia must still state the actual physical identity clearly and early.

Overview Paragraph 2 must not present a variety, trade name, rock, mineral aggregate, composite, synthetic, fossil, or treated material as a separate natural mineral species.

---

## 7. Parent and Variety Relationships

Parent and variety pages may coexist.

Rules:

- identify the parent relationship immediately
- avoid repeating full family-level geology
- explain what is visually, historically, commercially, or traditionally distinct
- cross-link related parent and variety entries where useful
- do not imply a separate mineral species when none exists

Examples of relationship framing:

- mineral variety of a broader species
- locality variety of Clear Quartz
- treated form of Quartz
- trade name for Labradorite
- rock made of several minerals
- fossilized organic material
- manufactured glass

## 12. Treatment and Provenance Policy

When treatment or identity varies by specimen:

- do not universalize
- distinguish common market treatment from confirmed treatment
- preserve provenance uncertainty
- state what can and cannot be determined visually
- avoid calling material fake without evidence
- place treatment detail in Identification or Market & Buying Notes
- keep Formation geological

Treatment flags belong in canonical production data where available.
