# Editorial and Research Standards v1.0

## The One Medical Boundary
Do not make claims a reader could reasonably interpret as health, medical, or psychological advice. Do not say a stone treats, heals, cures, prevents, or diagnoses any condition. Do not make claims about specific organs, hormones, circulation, fertility, addiction, or mental health treatment.

Examples that are never permitted:
- "Carnelian boosts circulation and supports the reproductive system"
- "Amethyst treats anxiety and helps with addiction recovery"
- "Selenite detoxifies the body and repairs cellular damage"
- "Rose Quartz supports heart health and regulates hormones"
- "Black Tourmaline protects against EMF radiation"

Everything else is permitted when it reflects genuine source consensus and is framed as traditional, practitioner-based, or commonly associated. Crystal traditions have their own honest language — use it directly and confidently.

## Voice
Write to be compelling and direct. "Carnelian is the stone of vitality and bold action" is better than "Carnelian is associated with vitality." Say what the stone is known for. Frame it as tradition when the claim needs grounding, but do not hedge everything into mush.

Permitted constructions (use freely, these are examples not a required list):
- "Carnelian is the stone of vitality and bold action"
- "Rose Quartz has a long tradition as the stone of love and compassion"
- "Black Tourmaline opens the root chakra and creates a strong energetic boundary"
- "Amethyst raises your vibration and quiets mental noise"
- "Selenite absorbs and clears heavy energy from a space"
- "Labradorite is traditionally used to protect the aura during spiritual work"

Do not present metaphysical claims as scientifically proven. Do not name sources in public copy. Do not use guru language, promotional superlatives, or em dashes.

## Required Counts
- 3 Property Pills
- 1 Best For
- 1 Use When sentence
- 2 Overview paragraphs
- 5 use cases
- 1 or 2 Primary themes, evidence-supported
- 0, 1, or 2 Secondary themes, evidence-supported
- 0, 1, or 2 Occasional Associations, evidence-supported
- 3 or 4 Collector Notes
- 2 Similar Energy
- 2 Pairs Well With

## Research Minimums
Metaphysical:
- standard stone: 6 to 10 approved sources
- thin-source stone: 4 to 6 approved sources
- A+ and A form the core consensus
- B may support but not override

Technical:
- 2 to 4 qualified mineralogical, gemological, museum, scientific, or institutional sources

## Theme Thresholds
- Primary: A+ support or strong consensus across at least 3 reputable sources
- Secondary: meaningful agreement across at least 2
- Occasional: supported but situational
- Omit weak, conflicting, unsafe, repetitive, or idiosyncratic claims

## Pairings
- Similar Energy is a practical alternative.
- Pairs Well With adds a complementary function.
- All 4 related stones must be unique.
- Reasons must explain the relationship, not merely list qualities.

## Stone-Name Repetition Control

The stone name must not be repeated mechanically throughout an entry.

Rules:

1. Do not begin multiple consecutive paragraphs, bullets, cards, theme descriptions, or comparison notes with the stone name.
2. In the Hero "Use When" section, never write "Use [stone] when" or "Reach for [stone] when." The section label already supplies that context. Begin directly with the circumstance or need.
3. After the stone has been clearly identified in a section, use natural alternatives where clarity allows:
   - it
   - this stone
   - this material
   - this variety
   - this quartz
   - the specimen
   - the emphasis here
   - direct sentence construction with no repeated subject
4. Keep the stone name only where needed for:
   - the first clear identity sentence
   - mineralogical precision
   - treatment terminology
   - trade names
   - comparisons where a pronoun would be ambiguous
5. Do not remove the name so aggressively that the prose becomes vague or awkward. The goal is natural variation, not avoidance at all costs.
6. Audit every completed entry for repeated sentence openings, especially:
   - "[Stone] is…"
   - "[Stone] supports…"
   - "[Stone] is associated…"
   - "[Stone] is chosen…"
   - "[Stone] pairs…"
7. No more than two nearby prose units should begin with the stone name.
8. The Hero, Energetic Themes, Related Stones, Collector Notes, and Mineral Profile require a dedicated repetition pass before approval.
9. Treat excessive name repetition as an editorial failure, even when all individual sentences are factually correct.
10. Preserve concise, natural prose. Do not replace repeated names with bloated abstractions.

### Clarification: Core Identity Words

Repetition control targets mechanical name repetition only — the stone's proper name (e.g., "Malachite," "Hematite") repeated as a sentence opener across consecutive prose units.

Core identity words that are central to the stone's meaning (e.g., love, protection, grounding, transformation, clarity) may recur naturally throughout an entry when the content requires them. Do not force artificial synonyms for these words in an attempt to avoid repetition. The goal is natural variation in sentence construction, not vocabulary avoidance.

### Use When — Hero Rule
- Begin directly with the situation or need.
- Never start with "Use [stone] when," "Reach for [stone] when," or equivalent phrasing.
- Target 3 lines preferred, 4 maximum.

## Editing
- Preserve approved copy during structural updates.
- Report factual concerns separately before changing public copy.
- Keep sections distinct.
- Never leave placeholders or unresolved notes.

## Formation Field Boundary

Formation contains only information about how the material forms geologically.

Formation may include:
- geological environment
- formative process
- crystallization, deposition, metamorphism, cooling, evaporation, or related natural processes
- resulting natural structure, habit, or aggregate form
- natural color-producing processes only when they are part of formation

Formation must not include:
- artificial treatments or commercial processing
- dyeing, heating, coating, irradiation, impregnation, stabilization, or filling performed after formation
- care or cleaning
- market availability or pricing
- imitations or identification guidance
- collector-quality advice
- trade-name discussion unless the name describes a natural formation habit essential to explaining formation

Move those subjects to the appropriate existing fields: Overview, Collector Context, Collector & Curiosity Notes, Care & Cleansing, or internal research notes.
