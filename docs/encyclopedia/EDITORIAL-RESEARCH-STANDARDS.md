# Editorial and Research Standards v1.0

## Voice
- Calm, clear, authoritative.
- Use metaphysical language only when common, useful, and source-supported.
- Prefer: `associated with`, `traditionally used`, `often chosen`, `commonly linked`.
- Do not present metaphysical claims as measured scientific mechanisms.
- Do not name sources in public copy.
- Do not use `magic`, guru language, promotional superlatives, or em dashes.

## Medical Boundaries
Do not claim stones diagnose, treat, cure, prevent, detoxify, or heal medical or psychological conditions.
Do not make addiction, anxiety-treatment, or sleep-treatment claims.

## Required Counts
- 3 Property Pills
- 1 Best For
- 1 Use When sentence
- 2 Overview paragraphs
- 5 use cases
- 1 or 2 Primary themes, evidence-supported
- 0, 1, or 2 Secondary themes, evidence-supported
- 0, 1, or 2 Occasional Associations, evidence-supported
- 3 or 4 Collector Notes
- 2 Similar Energy
- 2 Pairs Well With

## Research Minimums
Metaphysical:
- standard stone: 6 to 10 approved sources
- thin-source stone: 4 to 6 approved sources
- A+ and A form the core consensus
- B may support but not override

Technical:
- 2 to 4 qualified mineralogical, gemological, museum, scientific, or institutional sources

## Theme Thresholds
- Primary: A+ support or strong consensus across at least 3 reputable sources
- Secondary: meaningful agreement across at least 2
- Occasional: supported but situational
- Omit weak, conflicting, unsafe, repetitive, or idiosyncratic claims

## Pairings
- Similar Energy is a practical alternative.
- Pairs Well With adds a complementary function.
- All 4 related stones must be unique.
- Reasons must explain the relationship, not merely list qualities.

## Stone-Name Repetition Control

The stone name must not be repeated mechanically throughout an entry.

Rules:

1. Do not begin multiple consecutive paragraphs, bullets, cards, theme descriptions, or comparison notes with the stone name.
2. In the Hero "Use When" section, never write "Use [stone] when" or "Reach for [stone] when." The section label already supplies that context. Begin directly with the circumstance or need.
3. After the stone has been clearly identified in a section, use natural alternatives where clarity allows:
   - it
   - this stone
   - this material
   - this variety
   - this quartz
   - the specimen
   - the emphasis here
   - direct sentence construction with no repeated subject
4. Keep the stone name only where needed for:
   - the first clear identity sentence
   - mineralogical precision
   - treatment terminology
   - trade names
   - comparisons where a pronoun would be ambiguous
5. Do not remove the name so aggressively that the prose becomes vague or awkward. The goal is natural variation, not avoidance at all costs.
6. Audit every completed entry for repeated sentence openings, especially:
   - "[Stone] is…"
   - "[Stone] supports…"
   - "[Stone] is associated…"
   - "[Stone] is chosen…"
   - "[Stone] pairs…"
7. No more than two nearby prose units should begin with the stone name.
8. The Hero, Energetic Themes, Related Stones, Collector Notes, and Mineral Profile require a dedicated repetition pass before approval.
9. Treat excessive name repetition as an editorial failure, even when all individual sentences are factually correct.
10. Preserve concise, natural prose. Do not replace repeated names with bloated abstractions.

### Use When — Hero Rule
- Begin directly with the situation or need.
- Never start with "Use [stone] when," "Reach for [stone] when," or equivalent phrasing.
- Target 3 lines preferred, 4 maximum.

## Formation Rule

The Formation field covers geology only. Treatment information does not belong in Formation.

- **Include:** how the stone forms, geological environment, color origin, crystal habits, relevant physical context.
- **Exclude:** treatment disclosures, market authenticity warnings, imitation notes, fraud flags.

Treatment and authenticity information belongs in the Collector Context paragraphs (quality indicators, identification, market pricing) or in Collector & Curiosity Notes — not in Formation.

This is a hard separation. Do not end a Formation paragraph with a treatment sentence even when the transition feels natural.

## Editing
- Preserve approved copy during structural updates.
- Report factual concerns separately before changing public copy.
- Keep sections distinct.
- Never leave placeholders or unresolved notes.
