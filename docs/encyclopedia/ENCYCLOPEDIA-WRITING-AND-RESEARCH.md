# Still Point Lapidary — Encyclopedia Writing and Research

**Status:** Draft for Christie review  
**Purpose:** Defines the research standards, evidence thresholds, claim framing, section jobs, and editorial voice for every encyclopedia stone entry.

---

## 1. Authority and Scope

This document controls:

- research quality and evidence requirements
- claim strength and qualification
- metaphysical voice and framing
- section-specific writing jobs
- repetition control
- distinction between geological fact, metaphysical consensus, and editorial synthesis
- editorial review standards

This document does **not** control:

- page structure or responsive behavior
- field counts or optionality
- database columns
- exact visual styling
- stone-specific locked production values
- final publication approval

Related authorities:

- `ENCYCLOPEDIA-PAGE-STRUCTURE.md` controls page order and placement
- `ENCYCLOPEDIA-CONTENT-FIELDS.md` controls fields, counts, and limits
- `ENCYCLOPEDIA-APPROVED-SOURCES.md` controls source eligibility
- `ENCYCLOPEDIA-DATABASE-REFERENCE.md` controls storage and mapping
- locked production data controls stone-specific structured values
- canonical MD controls approved public copy

When approved sources materially conflict or a claim cannot be framed confidently without overstating the evidence, stop and ask Christie or Dustin.

---

## 2. Editorial Voice

The encyclopedia voice is:

- clear
- warm
- authoritative
- curious
- specific
- concise
- human
- compelling without being promotional

Write for an intelligent, curious adult who may not know mineralogy or crystal traditions.

The goal is not merely to inform. Each page should create enough recognition, surprise, or interest that the reader wants to keep exploring.

Prefer:

- direct sentences
- vivid but controlled imagery
- specific distinctions
- strong verbs
- memorable phrasing
- clean explanation
- confident editorial judgment

Avoid:

- vague wellness language
- generic uplift
- padded qualifiers
- repetitive source hedging
- textbook stiffness
- retailer copy
- mystical grandiosity
- promotional superlatives
- guru language
- em dashes in public copy

---

## 3. Metaphysical Language

### 3.1 Directness Is the Default

Use direct, confident language for well-supported metaphysical consensus.

Preferred:

- “Hematite is the stone of grounding when your thoughts have outrun your body.”
- “Carnelian is the stone of vitality and bold action.”
- “Moonstone is the stone of intuition during change.”

Avoid automatic hedging such as:

- “is commonly associated with”
- “is often linked to”
- “is traditionally believed to”
- “is sometimes chosen for”

These constructions are not forbidden, but they should appear only when the claim genuinely requires qualification.

### 3.2 Confidence Must Match Evidence

Qualify when a claim is:

- weakly supported
- disputed
- secondary rather than central
- culturally specific
- historically limited
- inconsistent across approved sources
- dependent on a narrow school of crystal tradition

Examples:

- “In some crystal traditions, Moonstone is also connected with fertility and lunar cycles.”
- “A smaller strand of sources links this variety with dreamwork.”
- “The association appears in modern crystal practice but is not broadly shared.”

Do not qualify merely because the topic is metaphysical.

### 3.3 Prohibited Framing

Do not present metaphysical claims as:

- scientific mechanisms
- medical facts
- psychological treatment
- diagnostic guidance
- guaranteed outcomes
- measurable energetic effects
- proof derived from physical mineral properties

Do not claim stones diagnose, treat, cure, prevent, detoxify, regulate, or heal medical or psychological conditions.

Do not use physical properties such as piezoelectricity as evidence of metaphysical action.

## 4. Research Model

Research begins from scratch unless Christie identifies an approved prior research record.

Research is conducted horizontally across a cohort by domain.

### Pass 1: Geological and Identity Research

Cover:

- accepted identity
- mineral species, variety, rock, composite, fossil, organic, synthetic, or trade-name status
- composition
- crystal system or structure where applicable
- hardness, cleavage, specific gravity, luster, transparency
- formation
- natural color origin
- treatments
- imitations
- locality
- collector and market distinctions
- specialist nomenclature questions

### Pass 2: Metaphysical Consensus

Cover:

- defining role
- chakra
- element
- zodiac
- central themes
- secondary themes
- occasional associations
- practical use cases
- historical or modern tradition where relevant

Planet is not researched for new production. Existing stored values may remain, but Planet is not a required field and is not displayed.

### Pass 3: Reader and Collector Usefulness

Cover:

- Why People Reach For It
- care and cleaning
- related stones
- pairings
- collector notes
- market confusion
- value drivers
- tactile or handling distinctions
- interesting cultural or scientific context

### Pass 4: Cohort Normalization

Normalize:

- terminology
- claim strength
- source quality
- field vocabulary
- treatment language
- identity framing
- theme thresholds
- locality detail
- market language
- repetition

Do not begin MD drafting until the cohort has completed all four passes.

## 5. Research Minimums

### 5.1 Metaphysical Research

Standard stone:

- 6–10 approved metaphysical sources

Thin-source stone:

- 4–6 approved metaphysical sources

Use Tier A+ and Tier A sources as the consensus core.

Tier B may support an established pattern but may not independently establish or override a major claim.

### 5.2 Technical Research

Use 2–4 qualified geological, mineralogical, gemological, museum, scientific, or institutional sources.

Use specialist verification where identity, naming, treatment, or composition requires it.

### 5.3 Thin-Source Handling

When strong source coverage is limited:

- reduce claim breadth
- reduce theme count
- omit weak optional fields
- preserve uncertainty
- avoid filling every possible slot
- flag unresolved identity or treatment issues

Thin sourcing is not permission to pad from weak retailers or unsupported summaries.

---

## 6. Evidence Thresholds

### Primary Energetic Theme

Requires:

- Tier A+ support, or
- strong agreement across at least 3 reputable approved sources

### Secondary Energetic Theme

Requires:

- meaningful agreement across at least 2 approved sources

### Occasional Association

Requires:

- real but situational support
- clear distinction from the central identity

### Omit a Claim When

- sources materially conflict
- the claim appears only once without broader support
- it is unsafe
- it duplicates a stronger theme
- it depends on a prohibited source
- it reads as medical or psychological advice
- it cannot be explained without overstating certainty

---

## 7. Distinguish Three Kinds of Content

Every research packet must distinguish:

### Geological Fact

Verifiable physical, chemical, structural, locality, treatment, or market information.

### Metaphysical Consensus

Patterns found across approved crystal-tradition sources.

### Editorial Synthesis

Still Point Lapidary’s concise explanation of how supported ideas fit together.

Editorial synthesis may clarify and connect. It may not invent a new claim.

---

## 8. Section Jobs and Writing Standards

Every section has one job. Content belongs where it serves that job best.

---

## 9. Hero: Signature Line

**Job:** Distill the stone into one memorable sentence.

**Voice:** Precise, evocative, restrained.

The line should feel specific enough that it could not be pasted onto several other stones.

Avoid:

- generic slogans
- property lists
- unexplained mystical language
- duplicate Best For or Use When phrasing

---

## 10. Hero: Best For

**Job:** State the primary practical use.

**Voice:** Direct noun phrase or gerund phrase.

Good:

- “Regaining stability, sharpening focus, and returning to practical action”

Avoid:

- full explanatory sentences
- “This stone is best for...”
- vague wording such as “personal growth and healing”

---

## 11. Hero: Use When

**Job:** Name the moment that brings someone to the stone.

**Voice:** Immediate, reader-centered, second-person in effect.

Begin with the circumstance.

Good:

- “Your mind is racing and you need to stop circling the same decision.”

Avoid:

- “Use Hematite when...”
- “Reach for Hematite when...”
- abstract mechanism language

---

## 12. Hero: Affirmation

**Job:** Give the reader a simple sentence to carry.

**Voice:** First person, present tense, natural.

It should sound like something a real person would say.

Avoid:

- poster language
- ornate spirituality
- promises
- vague positivity

---

## 13. Overview Paragraph 1

**Job:** Make the reader understand why people care about this stone.

**Voice:** Warm, confident, compelling.

Open with the stone name and defining role.

Develop one central idea rather than stacking several properties.

Preferred pattern:

1. direct identity
2. why it matters
3. how its themes work together
4. one useful nuance or contrast

Example:

> Hematite is the stone of grounding when your thoughts have outrun your body. Its appeal is practical rather than dreamy: come back to where you are, notice what is real, and take the next solid step.

Avoid:

- a list of traits disguised as prose
- weak hedge chains
- opening with composition
- repeating the Signature Line verbatim

---

## 14. Overview Paragraph 2

**Job:** Make the material itself interesting.

**Voice:** A smart friend explaining the most surprising physical truth.

Open with:

- a descriptive noun phrase, or
- the most compelling physical fact

Do not open with the stone name.

Preferred:

> A metallic gray iron oxide that reveals a reddish-brown streak, Hematite often gives away its identity only when its polished surface is tested.

Avoid:

> Hematite is an iron oxide mineral with the formula Fe₂O₃.

Cover only the physical facts most useful to a general reader:

- what it is
- what makes it distinctive
- appearance
- formation
- treatment
- trade-name or authenticity issues

Do not duplicate the fact table sentence by sentence.

---

## 15. Why People Reach For It

**Job:** Make the reader feel recognized.

Each row should answer:

> What is happening in my life that makes this stone relevant?

Voice:

- specific
- emotionally observant
- practical
- reader-centered

Good:

- “When everyone around you has an opinion and you have lost track of your own.”
- “When you know what needs doing but keep circling instead of beginning.”

Avoid:

- metaphysical mechanism language
- “supports you in”
- “helps promote”
- generic intention labels without a real situation
- five variations of the same emotional state

---

## 16. Energetic Themes

**Job:** Give a fast personality sketch.

Voice:

- direct
- compact
- action-first
- scannable

Start with the action or effect.

Good:

- “Draws scattered attention back to the body and what is immediately in front of you.”
- “Steadies without stiffening, keeping presence responsive rather than rigid.”

Avoid openings such as:

- “It...”
- “Its energy...”
- “This stone...”
- the stone name
- “Traditionally associated with...”

One sentence is enough.

---

## 17. Mineral Facts

**Job:** Provide clean reference data.

Voice:

- factual
- concise
- neutral

No editorial flourish inside the facts table.

Do not force true-mineral labels onto rocks, composites, organics, fossils, synthetics, mineraloids, or trade-name mixtures.

---

## 18. Formation

**Job:** Explain how the material forms geologically.

Voice:

- clear
- precise
- vivid where the science allows

Formation covers geology only.

Include:

- geological environment
- natural growth or formation process
- natural color origin
- crystal habit or structure when relevant

Exclude:

- treatment
- market warnings
- imitation
- fraud
- care
- metaphysical claims

Do not end a Formation paragraph with a treatment sentence.

---

## 19. Quality Indicators

**Job:** Help the reader recognize a strong specimen.

Voice:

- knowledgeable
- practical
- direct
- lightly opinionated

Explain what actually matters for that material:

- color
- clarity
- luster
- optical effect
- crystal habit
- surface
- matrix
- provenance
- size
- condition
- form-specific distinctions

When different forms have different quality standards, explain the forms first.

---

## 20. Identification

**Job:** Help the reader avoid confusion or misrepresentation.

Cover:

- lookalikes
- misleading trade names
- treatments
- synthetics
- composites
- common imitations
- useful physical tests
- provenance limits

Voice:

- honest
- practical
- protective without alarmism

Do not move treatment warnings into Formation.

---

## 21. Locality Variations

**Job:** Explain meaningful source-based differences.

Include only when locality changes:

- color
- clarity
- crystal habit
- quality
- matrix
- collector reputation
- market value

Do not write a geography lesson.

Omit when the distinctions are weak or unsupported.

---

## 22. Physical Handling

**Job:** Tell the reader what the stone is like to hold.

Useful details may include:

- notable weight
- surface texture
- edge fragility
- unusual smoothness
- porosity
- sharp crystal faces
- tactile contrast between forms

Omit when the stone feels ordinary and there is nothing useful to add.

---

## 23. Market & Buying Notes

**Job:** Calibrate expectations before someone shops.

Cover:

- common versus rare
- entry-level versus collector quality
- what raises value
- what does not automatically raise value
- provenance
- availability
- market confusion

Voice:

- informed
- direct
- realistic

Do not create live price tables or pretend prices are stable.

Do not repeat Quality Indicators or Identification.

---

## 24. Collector & Curiosity Notes

**Job:** Deliver the most interesting facts on the page.

Each note needs:

- a hook in the title
- a payoff in the body
- a distinct subject

Voice:

- warm
- surprising
- compact
- memorable

Good titles:

- “Gray outside, red underneath”
- “Art Nouveau’s obsession”
- “Magnetic hematite usually is not Hematite”

Avoid generic titles such as:

- “History”
- “Interesting Fact”
- “Collector Note”
- “A Sacred Stone”

Every note must earn its place.

---

## 25. Care & Cleaning

**Job:** Give practical care guidance quickly.

Voice:

- direct
- warm
- confident
- evidence-based

Each category uses one sentence.

Do not ban words such as `may` or `can` when genuine uncertainty depends on:

- treatment
- coating
- matrix
- fracture
- specimen condition
- provenance

Use direct certainty where the evidence supports it.

Avoid:

- spiritual cleansing methods
- invented warnings
- vague caution
- legalistic tone
- overstatement

---

## 26. Related Stones: Similar Energy

**Job:** Clarify personality through comparison.

Structure:

1. shared quality
2. meaningful difference

Good:

> Shares the grounding and steadiness, but feels gentler, more spacious, and less focused on physical embodiment.

Do not merely list shared traits.

---

## 27. Related Stones: Pairs Well With

**Job:** Explain why someone would use both stones.

Structure:

1. what the main stone does
2. what the paired stone adds, extends, or balances

Good:

> Adds warmth and relational openness when clear boundaries need a softer emotional expression.

Do not force a “missing piece” narrative when the relationship is simply additive.

---

## 28. Stone-Name Repetition

The stone name should feel intentional, not mechanical.

Rules:

1. Use the stone name in the first clear identity sentence.
2. Do not begin several nearby paragraphs, rows, or notes with the stone name.
3. Do not begin Use When, Energetic Themes, or related-stone reasons with the stone name.
4. Use direct sentence construction where possible.
5. Use `it`, `this stone`, `this material`, or `this variety` only when they sound natural.
6. Keep the stone name when needed for mineralogical precision.
7. Avoid repeated openings such as:
   - “[Stone] is...”
   - “[Stone] supports...”
   - “[Stone] helps...”
   - “[Stone] is associated...”
8. Do not replace clear language with awkward abstractions merely to avoid repetition.
9. Perform a dedicated repetition pass before approval.
10. Treat visible repetition as an editorial defect.

---

## 29. Identity and Exception Handling

Do not force every entry through true-mineral assumptions.

Flag before drafting:

- rocks
- composites
- mineraloids
- organics
- synthetics
- fossils
- treated materials
- disputed trade names
- mixtures sold under one market name
- locality varieties
- commercial names
- materials requiring specialist nomenclature review

Overview Paragraph 2 must establish the actual identity immediately.

Examples:

- variety of a broader species
- rock made of several minerals
- treated quartz
- fossilized organic material
- manufactured glass
- commercial name for another material

Familiar market names may remain as page titles when approved, but the copy must correct the identity clearly and early.

---

## 30. Treatment and Authenticity

Treatment claims require strong verification.

When treatment varies by specimen or provenance:

- do not universalize
- state what is common
- distinguish natural and treated possibilities
- explain what can and cannot be determined visually
- direct uncertainty to Identification or Market & Buying Notes

Do not accuse a product of being fake without evidence.

Do not describe a treated commercial material as naturally occurring when it is not.

---

## 31. Localities

Use localities that are:

- commercially important
- historically significant
- collector-relevant
- strongly supported by approved research

Do not list every occurrence.

Use concise public-facing geography.

Preferred abbreviations include:

- USA
- DRC

Locality facts must not be invented from retailer listings.

---

## 32. Research Records

Internal research records must preserve:

- exact sources
- evidence strength
- source disagreements
- rejected claims
- treatment uncertainty
- identity ambiguity
- source tier
- thin-source flags
- reasoning behind omissions

Public copy does not name sources unless editorially necessary.

Research notes remain separate from public copy.

---

## 33. Review Standard

Before MD approval, review for:

- factual accuracy
- correct material identity
- evidence support
- claim strength
- directness without overstatement
- section-job compliance
- repetition
- distinct themes and use cases
- treatment placement
- geological accuracy
- useful collector guidance
- concise writing
- no padding
- no medical or psychological advice
- no source names in public copy
- no placeholders

A page is not ready because every field is filled.

A page is ready when every included field earns its place.

---

## 34. Editing Rules

- Preserve approved meaning during structural edits.
- Separate factual corrections from stylistic improvements.
- Do not rewrite approved copy during technical work without permission.
- Do not move content into the wrong section merely to solve layout.
- Fix the layout or the specific overlong field instead.
- Do not pad weak optional fields.
- Do not silently normalize away meaningful identity or treatment nuance.
- Flag contradictions before choosing a side.

---

## 35. Approval and Change Control

Research and writing-standard changes require:

1. Christie or Dustin approval
2. update to this document
3. review of any active cohort affected by the change
4. targeted revision of approved MD only when the change materially affects content

This document reflects current standards only.

Do not preserve superseded rules in an amendment archive.
