## Overview

Tiger's Eye is the stone of grounded confidence, chosen when courage and judgment must operate together. Its energy is bold without being careless, helping ambition stay answerable to facts, consequences, and personal integrity. It suits decisions involving authority, money, visibility, or calculated risk because it does not merely ask whether you can act. It asks whether the action is wise, proportionate, and truly yours. Its central lesson is self-command: hold your nerve, see clearly, and use power without letting power use you.

A chatoyant quartz-rich rock, Tiger's Eye displays a moving band of light created by parallel fibrous structures preserved within the material. Golden-brown color develops as iron-bearing fibers alter and oxidize, while bluer material is commonly sold as Hawk's Eye. Older accounts described a simple one-for-one replacement of crocidolite by Quartz, but modern research indicates a more complicated history of overlapping Quartz growth, fibrous mineral development, and alteration. The optical eye is internal structure, not a surface coating.

### Formation

Tiger's Eye forms within iron-rich environments where Quartz and fibrous iron-bearing minerals develop, alter, and become intergrown in parallel bands. Oxidation converts bluer precursor material toward golden and brown tones while preserving the aligned texture responsible for chatoyancy. Its formation is more complex than a simple complete pseudomorph and may involve simultaneous growth and replacement processes.

### Quality Indicators

Fine Tiger's Eye shows a strong, centered, mobile eye that travels cleanly across the polished surface. Saturated golden-brown color, sharp contrast, balanced banding, smooth polish, and minimal distracting fractures increase desirability. Cut orientation is crucial: even excellent rough can produce a weak eye when the face is not aligned correctly with the internal fibers.

### Identification

Natural Tiger's Eye shows a shifting band that moves with the viewing angle and follows the stone's internal fibrous structure. Fiber-optic glass may look unnaturally uniform, display repeated artificial bands, or show mold and surface clues inconsistent with stone. Red Tiger's Eye is commonly heat-treated, and golden material may be confused with dyed glass, resin composites, or poorly disclosed reconstructed products.

### Locality Variations

South Africa is the principal commercial source and produces large quantities of golden, blue, and mixed banded material, including dramatic rock slabs from ancient iron formations. Australian and Namibian occurrences can show distinctive color and banding, while smaller deposits elsewhere may be valued more for locality interest than mass-market availability.

### Physical Handling

The chatoyant eye appears and disappears as the stone rotates, so a stationary photograph may reveal only one favorable angle. Polished pieces are generally durable, but visible fractures, thin slabs, and mixed iron-rich bands can create localized weaknesses that deserve careful handling.

## Market & Buying Notes

Tiger's Eye is widely available, but quality depends heavily on orientation, movement of the eye, color saturation, banding, polish, fracture condition, and treatment disclosure. Fiber-optic glass and resin products can imitate chatoyancy, while red material is often heat-treated and should not be assumed natural. A strong photograph taken at one precise angle may overstate how consistently the eye appears. Collector value rises for exceptional natural form, unusual locality, dramatic banded slabs, and finely centered chatoyant cuts.

## Care & Cleaning

### Cleaning
Wash gently with lukewarm water, mild soap, and a soft cloth, avoiding abrasives, steam, and harsh chemicals.

### Water
Brief washing is generally safe for solid untreated pieces, but avoid soaking fractured, glued, or stabilized material.

### Light & Heat
Normal indoor light is stable, while intense heat and rapid temperature change can damage fractures or alter treated material.

### Storage
Store separately from harder stones and protect visible fractures, thin slabs, and polished faces from impact.
