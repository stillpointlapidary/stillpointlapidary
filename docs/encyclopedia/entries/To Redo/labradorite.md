## Overview

Labradorite is a calcium-sodium plagioclase feldspar known for labradorescence, the shifting play of blue, green, gold, orange, or violet produced when light interacts with microscopic internal layers. The body color is often gray, brownish, or dark, making the flash highly angle-dependent. Finnish Spectrolite and white Labradorite sold as Rainbow Moonstone are important market distinctions.

Labradorite is the stone of transformation, intuition, protection, and self-discovery. Its changing optical effect offers a natural metaphor for perspective: more becomes visible when angle, attention, and context change. It is especially powerful during reinvention or spiritual exploration, when growth requires entering uncertainty without abandoning discernment.

### Formation

Labradorite crystallizes as a calcium-rich plagioclase feldspar in igneous rocks. Slow cooling allows microscopic internal layers to develop, and light interference within those layers produces labradorescence.

### Common Localities

### Collector Context

Look for strong, well-positioned flash, broad color coverage, an attractive body tone, and cutting that presents the optical effect clearly. Surface scratches, cleavage fractures, dead angles, and poorly oriented polish reduce value.

Rainbow Moonstone is usually white Labradorite rather than true orthoclase Moonstone, while Spectrolite refers to vivid Finnish material. Glass imitations may mimic color but lack the same internal structure and natural directional flash.

Labradorite is widely available in cabochons, carvings, palm stones, slabs, and jewelry. Price rises with flash intensity, color range, coverage, orientation, polish, condition, size, and documented Finnish Spectrolite provenance.

## Collector & Curiosity Notes

### Finnish Spectrolite Discovery
Spectrolite was identified in Finland’s Ylämaa region after unusually vivid Labradorite was encountered there during the twentieth century. The name was introduced for this exceptionally colorful Finnish material, whose documented locality remains central to its collector identity.

### Labradorescence
The color play is not surface pigment. It comes from light interacting with microscopic internal layers, so the flash appears, shifts, or disappears as the viewing angle changes.

### Finnish Spectrolite
Spectrolite is the trade name for vivid Finnish Labradorite with especially broad, saturated color play. Provenance is part of its collector appeal.

### Rainbow Moonstone
Much of the white material sold as Rainbow Moonstone is actually Labradorite. It belongs to plagioclase feldspar rather than the orthoclase material traditionally called Moonstone.

## Care & Cleansing

### Cleaning
Use mild soap, lukewarm water, and a soft cloth; avoid aggressive brushing around fractures or edges.

### Water
Brief water contact is generally safe, but avoid prolonged soaking when fractures, glue, fillers, or attached matrix are present.

### Light & Heat
Ordinary light is safe. Avoid strong heat, steam, and sudden temperature change, which may stress cleavage planes.

### Storage
Store separately from harder stones and protect from sharp impact because perfect cleavage makes the material vulnerable to splitting.
