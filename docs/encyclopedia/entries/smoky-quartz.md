## Overview

Smoky Quartz is a transparent to translucent brown, gray, or nearly black variety of crystalline quartz. Its color develops when natural radiation interacts with aluminum-related defects in the crystal lattice, although commercial irradiation can produce a similar result. Very dark material may be called Morion, while warm Scottish specimens from the Cairngorm region have a distinct collector identity.

Smoky Quartz is the stone of grounding, protection, practical awareness, and turning heavy experiences into steadier presence. Its Root emphasis keeps reflection connected to the body, ordinary responsibilities, and concrete action. Its transmutation tradition centers on meeting intensity without becoming unmoored and finding a more workable relationship to what feels heavy.

### Formation

Smoky Quartz forms in quartz-bearing geological settings including granitic pegmatites, hydrothermal veins, and metamorphic rocks. Its smoky color develops when natural radiation alters aluminum-related defects within the growing quartz lattice.

### Collector Context

Look for pleasing transparency, even or naturally zoned smoky color, intact crystal faces, and good condition. Deep color can be attractive, but darkness alone does not establish rarity or natural origin.

Morion, Cairngorm, irradiated quartz, and very dark glass can all create market confusion. Treatment cannot be established from color alone, so provenance and stronger gemological evidence matter when natural coloration affects price.

Common polished and tumbled material is widely available and affordable. Fine natural crystals, attractive zoning, large intact specimens, notable locality provenance, and documented Scottish Cairngorm material may command higher prices.

## Collector & Curiosity Notes

### Cairngorm and Scottish Jewelry
Warm smoky quartz from Scotland’s Cairngorm region became a signature material in Scottish jewelry, appearing in brooches, Highland dress ornaments, and designs combining native quartz with thistles, Scottish gold, or freshwater pearls.

### Morion
Morion is a traditional name for very dark brown to nearly black Smoky Quartz. It is not a separate mineral species, and darkness alone does not reveal whether the color is natural or induced.

### Color from Irradiation
The smoky color develops through radiation-related changes in the quartz lattice involving aluminum defects. Commercial irradiation can create or intensify a similar appearance.

### Color Can Fade
Sustained heat or strong light may lighten some specimens. The degree of change varies, so display conditions matter more for deeply colored or treated material.

## Care & Cleansing

### Cleaning
Wash with mild soap, lukewarm water, and a soft cloth or brush; dry thoroughly.

### Water
Brief water contact is safe for untreated quartz. Use caution with glued components, fillers, coatings, or attached matrix.

### Light & Heat
Avoid sustained heat and prolonged intense light, which may lighten color in some natural or treated specimens.

### Storage
Store separately or wrap points and edges; quartz can scratch softer stones and may chip under sharp impact.
