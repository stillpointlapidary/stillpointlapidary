## Overview

Clear Quartz is one of the most widely used stones in crystal practice, valued for amplification, clarity, and intention-setting. Its broad chakra association and comparatively neutral energetic identity make it adaptable: it can stand alone as a focal stone or work alongside crystals with more specialized emotional, grounding, or spiritual themes.

Its traditions often describe it as responsive to the purpose brought to it. Rather than treating this as literal memory storage, Still Point understands Clear Quartz as a symbolic anchor for sustained attention and deliberate practice. This makes it useful in meditation, crystal grids, personal rituals, and paired-stone work where the goal is to reinforce a clearly chosen direction.

## Mineral Profile

Clear Quartz, also called rock crystal when transparent, is the colorless to milky-white variety of crystalline quartz. It is composed of silicon dioxide and crystallizes in the trigonal system, commonly forming six-sided prismatic crystals with pyramidal terminations. Quartz occurs in igneous, metamorphic, and sedimentary environments and is one of the most abundant minerals in Earth’s continental crust.

Quartz has a Mohs hardness of 7, no true cleavage, and a conchoidal fracture. Transparent material may contain veils, fractures, fluid inclusions, mineral inclusions, or growth features that affect clarity without changing its mineral identity. Clear Quartz can also be synthetically grown for industrial and gem use, and natural pieces may be coated, irradiated, or otherwise treated for decorative effects.

Quartz is piezoelectric, meaning mechanical pressure can create an electrical charge across the crystal. This documented physical property supports its use in oscillators, clocks, watches, radios, and other electronic devices, but it should not be used as scientific proof of metaphysical amplification. Clean with mild soap, warm water, and a soft cloth. Avoid strong impacts, sustained high heat, and harsh chemical cleaners.

## Collector Notes

### Rock Crystal
Transparent colorless quartz is traditionally called rock crystal. The term distinguishes clear crystalline material from milky quartz and from colored varieties such as Amethyst, Citrine, Smoky Quartz, and Rose Quartz.

### Quartz in Technology
Pierre and Paul-Jacques Curie documented piezoelectricity in quartz and other crystals in 1880. Quartz’s stable vibration under electrical stimulation later made it important in clocks, watches, radios, pressure instruments, and electronic frequency control.

### Crystal Skull Claims
Quartz crystal skulls were often promoted as pre-Columbian artifacts, but studied examples lack secure archaeological provenance. Smithsonian research indicates that known specimens were manufactured in the nineteenth century or later using modern lapidary tools.

### Ancient Ice Belief
The Greek word *krystallos* referred to ice, and some classical writers believed rock crystal was water frozen so completely that it could never melt. The idea reflects its icy transparency rather than geological knowledge.

## Treatments, Trade Names, Imitations, and Care

- Rock crystal is a traditional name for transparent colorless quartz.
- Natural Clear Quartz may contain veils, fractures, mineral inclusions, or fluid inclusions.
- Synthetic quartz is widely produced for technological and some gem uses.
- Decorative quartz may be coated or treated, especially material sold under modern trade names such as aura quartz.
- Mild soap, warm water, and a soft cloth are suitable for routine cleaning.
- Strong impacts may chip points and edges despite quartz’s hardness.
- Sustained high heat and harsh chemicals should be avoided.
