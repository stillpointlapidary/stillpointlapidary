## Overview

Unakite is the stone of emotional integration, chosen when a present reaction seems to carry an older story inside it. Its steady character supports the patient work of noticing patterns, naming what belongs to the past, and making room for a different response now. Heart and Third Eye themes meet here: feeling is neither dismissed nor allowed to become the only source of truth. Instead, emotion, memory, insight, and choice are brought into conversation so understanding can become lived change rather than another idea left on the page.

A mottled green-and-pink rock formed through the alteration of granite, Unakite is composed chiefly of green Epidote, pink potassium feldspar, and Quartz. Hydrothermal fluids transform portions of the original feldspar into Epidote while preserving enough pink feldspar and pale Quartz to create its characteristic patchwork appearance. It has no single chemical formula or crystal system as a rock. Grain size, color balance, pits, and uneven polishing vary because its component minerals respond differently to weathering and cutting.

### Formation

Unakite forms when hydrothermal fluids alter granitic rock, converting portions of feldspar into green Epidote while leaving pink potassium feldspar and Quartz visible. The result is a coarse-grained patchwork whose colors reflect separate mineral components rather than a surface stain. Variation in fluid chemistry and degree of alteration produces different balances of green, pink, and pale Quartz.

### Quality Indicators

Desirable Unakite shows clear contrast between green Epidote and pink feldspar, an attractive distribution of color, stable texture, and a polish that does not expose excessive pits or undercut grains. Some collectors prefer evenly balanced color, while others value dramatic green-dominant or pink-dominant patterns. Thin carvings and heavily fractured pieces deserve closer inspection because mixed minerals can wear and break unevenly.

### Identification

Classic Unakite contains visible green Epidote, pink potassium feldspar, and Quartz in an interlocking granular texture. Not every pink-and-green rock meets that definition, and dyed stone may reveal color concentrated in pores or fractures. Serpentine mixtures, green-and-pink decorative rocks, and material marketed simply as “Epidote in feldspar” should be evaluated by composition rather than color resemblance alone.

### Locality Variations

Material from the Appalachian region of the USA has historical significance and often displays the classic coarse pink-and-green pattern. Commercial lapidary material from South Africa, Brazil, China, and Zimbabwe varies in grain size, color balance, fracture density, and polish response, with locality names sometimes used more broadly than firm provenance supports.

### Physical Handling

Unakite feels granular beneath a polish, and small pits or uneven areas may reflect natural differences among Epidote, feldspar, and Quartz. Corners and thin carvings can chip where mineral boundaries create weak points, while solid rounded forms are generally suitable for ordinary handling.

## Market & Buying Notes

Unakite is common and generally affordable, with appeal driven by color contrast, pattern balance, polish, carving quality, size, and credible locality. The name may be applied loosely to other pink-and-green rocks, so composition matters more than color alone. Neon hues, pigment gathered in fractures, or unnaturally uniform color should prompt dye questions. Resin filling may be used on fractured carvings, and photographs can conceal pitting or undercut grains that are easier to see in moving light.

## Care & Cleaning

### Cleaning
Use lukewarm water, mild soap, and a soft cloth, avoiding acids, harsh chemicals, steam, and ultrasonic cleaning.

### Water
Brief washing is generally acceptable for solid polished pieces, but avoid prolonged soaking of porous, fractured, filled, or matrix-rich material.

### Light & Heat
Ordinary indoor conditions are stable, while strong heat and sudden temperature change can stress the rock's different mineral components.

### Storage
Store separately from harder stones and protect corners, thin carvings, and natural pits from impact and abrasion.
