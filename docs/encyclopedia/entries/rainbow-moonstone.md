## Overview

Rainbow Moonstone is the stone of receptive intuition during change, especially when the old map no longer fits and the next direction has not become obvious. Its luminous quality suits dreamwork, symbolic reflection, synchronicity, and decisions that need patience rather than another round of forceful analysis. It also carries a protective edge: remain open, but notice which feelings, expectations, and impressions actually belong to you. Its strongest work happens in the pause where uncertainty becomes listening instead of panic.

A transparent to translucent white variety of Labradorite, Rainbow Moonstone is plagioclase feldspar rather than true orthoclase Moonstone. Its blue and multicolored flash is labradorescence, produced when light interacts with microscopic intergrowth structures inside the feldspar. Careful orientation during cutting is essential because the flash can disappear when viewed from the wrong angle. The name is firmly established in the gem trade, but the page must preserve the distinction from true Moonstone and from yellow Golden Labradorite.

### Formation

Rainbow Moonstone forms as Labradorite within igneous rocks and pegmatitic environments where feldspar compositions separate into microscopic intergrowth layers during cooling. These internal layers scatter and interfere with light, producing blue or multicolored labradorescence. The optical effect depends on composition, layer thickness, orientation, and the quality of the cut.

### Quality Indicators

Strong material combines bright, well-positioned flash with appealing transparency, minimal distracting fractures, clean polish, and a face oriented to display the effect through a useful viewing range. Full-spectrum flash may command a premium, but a vivid blue sheen on a clean body can be equally compelling. Dead zones, surface-reaching fractures, weak orientation, and flash visible from only an impractical angle reduce quality.

### Identification

Rainbow Moonstone is white Labradorite, not true Moonstone and not Golden Labradorite. Labradorescence often appears as blue, green, yellow, orange, or rainbow flashes across a transparent to milky body, while true Moonstone shows softer adularescence associated with a different feldspar intergrowth. Opalite glass, coated glass, synthetic effects, and seller photographs taken only at the strongest angle can misrepresent appearance. Always evaluate video or multiple angles when flash coverage matters.

### Locality Variations

Indian material dominates the commercial market and ranges from milky white cabochon rough to highly transparent pieces with vivid blue or rainbow flash. Madagascar and Sri Lanka can produce clean feldspar with attractive optical effects, while Tanzanian material occasionally appears in gem and collector markets. Provenance claims should be treated cautiously unless supported by reliable documentation.

### Physical Handling

The flash shifts or disappears as the stone turns, so apparent quality changes dramatically with angle and light source. Feldspar cleavage and surface-reaching fractures make thin cabochons, points, and carvings more vulnerable than their polished finish suggests.

## Market & Buying Notes

Rainbow Moonstone varies widely in transparency, flash color, coverage, fracture visibility, and cutting quality. Strong full-face flash and clean body material command higher prices, but edited photographs can make a narrow viewing angle look universal. Confirm that the seller understands the material is white Labradorite, request video through several angles, and inspect surface-reaching fractures or fills. “Blue flash,” “rainbow flash,” and “white Labradorite” describe market distinctions within the same broader identity, not separate mineral species.

## Care & Cleaning

### Cleaning
Use warm soapy water and a soft cloth, avoiding steam, ultrasonic cleaning, abrasives, and harsh chemicals.

### Water
Brief washing is acceptable for intact material, but avoid soaking fractured, filled, glued, or mounted pieces.

### Light & Heat
Ordinary light is stable, while high heat and sudden temperature changes can exploit feldspar cleavage and cause damage.

### Storage
Wrap separately and protect from blows, pressure, and contact with harder stones.
