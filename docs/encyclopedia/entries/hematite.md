## Overview

Hematite is the stone of grounding when your thoughts have outrun your body and everything feels harder to hold onto. Its tradition is practical and unsentimental: come back to where you are, notice what is real, establish your footing, and deal with the next thing in front of you. Root Chakra and Earth associations give it stability, while Mars adds resolve and the willingness to act. This is not passive stillness. It is composure with weight behind it.

A metallic gray iron oxide that leaves a reddish-brown streak, this mineral often reveals its true identity only when the surface is scratched across an unglazed plate. Its substantial weight and reflective luster make polished pieces feel unmistakably dense, while natural forms range from mirror-bright plates and rounded botryoidal masses to earthy red material. Most natural specimens are untreated. Strongly magnetic products sold as magnetic hematite are usually manufactured iron-based material marketed as hematine.

---

## Formation

Some Hematite grows in vast banded iron formations, while other material develops in hydrothermal veins, metamorphic rocks, volcanic settings, and oxidation zones. Its iron-oxide chemistry creates both the metallic gray surface of many specimens and the reddish-brown color that appears when the mineral is powdered. It can form as bright plates, rounded botryoidal masses, earthy material, or replacements after earlier minerals.

### Collector’s Guide

Hematite comes in enough distinct forms that quality means something different depending on what you're buying. Across all of them, density, surface integrity, and authenticity of form matter most. Specular plates should have strong mirror luster and clean edges; botryoidal pieces should show rounded, unblemished growth; polished shapes should feel notably heavy with an even finish. Earthy red material and martite pseudomorphs are a different category entirely, with value rooted in geological character rather than shine.

Natural Hematite leaves a reddish-brown streak and is only weakly magnetic. Strongly magnetic beads, carvings, and polished shapes are commonly manufactured iron-based material sold as hematine or magnetic hematite. Magnetite is naturally more magnetic, while molded metallic products and dark coatings can imitate the look.

Ordinary polished pieces are widely available and usually affordable. Prices rise for exceptional crystal form, bright specular luster, undamaged botryoidal growth, unusual pseudomorphs, historic localities, or strong provenance. A large piece is not automatically a rare or important one.

## Collector & Curiosity Notes

### Gray outside, red underneath

A metallic gray specimen leaves a reddish-brown streak. That hidden color is one of the quickest clues to Hematite’s iron-oxide identity.

### Magnetic hematite usually is not Hematite

Natural material is only weakly magnetic. Strongly magnetic beads and carvings are commonly manufactured iron-based products sold as hematine or magnetic hematite.

### One mineral, many personalities

Collectors encounter mirror-bright specularite, rounded kidney ore, botryoidal masses, earthy red ochre, and martite pseudomorphs after Magnetite.

### Red ochre came first

Long before metallic specimens became popular in jewelry and mineral collections, finely powdered Hematite supplied durable red ochre pigment.

## Care & Cleansing

### Cleaning

Wipe it with a soft dry or slightly damp cloth, use mild soap only when needed, and dry it promptly.

### Water

Keep water contact brief and avoid soaking pieces with fractures, porous matrix, or attached labels.

### Light & Heat

Ordinary light is fine, but keep it away from strong heat and rapid temperature changes.

### Storage

Store it separately from softer stones and keep mixed-mineral or matrix specimens dry and protected from impact.
