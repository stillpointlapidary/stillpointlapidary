## Overview

Black Tourmaline sold in the crystal market is most commonly schorl, the iron-rich species of the tourmaline mineral group. A complex sodium iron aluminum borosilicate, it crystallizes in the trigonal system and forms predominantly in granitic pegmatites, granites, and metamorphic rocks worldwide. Schorl is the dominant tourmaline species in most natural geological environments, and it is the form of tourmaline encountered most frequently in the crystal market as tumbled stones, rough specimens, natural points, and wands.

Its metaphysical traditions are among the most consistent of any protective stone. Black Tourmaline is associated with the Earth Star Chakra and the Root Chakra, and its reputation centers on grounding, boundary-setting, and clearing unwanted or heavy energy from personal fields, spaces, and objects. People reach for it during periods of energetic overwhelm, when boundaries feel depleted or porous, when sensitivity to others' energy is high, or simply as a daily anchor that maintains a stable, contained sense of self through demanding circumstances.

## Mineral Profile

Black Tourmaline is schorl, the most abundant species in the tourmaline mineral group. Its chemical composition is NaFe2+3Al6Si6O18(BO3)3(OH)4 — sodium iron aluminum borosilicate — and it crystallizes in the trigonal system. It forms predominantly in granitic pegmatites and granites, as well as in metamorphic rocks and hydrothermal veins. Its consistently dark color is closely tied to its iron-rich composition. The crystal system is correctly trigonal (rhombohedral); older sources and many popular references describe tourmaline as hexagonal, which reflects a historical classification grouping that has since been revised in mineralogical practice.

With a Mohs hardness of 6 to 7.5, Black Tourmaline is generally durable for handling and display. It is not typically treated. Common imitations include black obsidian, black glass, jet, black kyanite, and morion (black quartz), which can be distinguished by their different optical properties, hardness, and internal character. Clean with mild soap and lukewarm water; avoid harsh chemicals. Store away from softer minerals that its edges could scratch.

## Collector Notes

### Schorl: The True Name
Most of the world's tourmaline is schorl. The iron-rich composition that makes it black also makes it the dominant tourmaline species in granite and pegmatite formation globally. Despite its abundance, schorl was long commercially secondary to the rarer colored tourmalines valued in gem cutting and jewelry.

### Naturally Piezoelectric
Tourmaline, including schorl, is naturally piezoelectric and pyroelectric: it generates a measurable electrical charge when compressed or heated. This property is sometimes referenced in the stone's protective reputation, though the energetic tradition is far broader in scope than the electrical phenomenon that inspired the comparison.

### The Striated Crystal Surface
Black Tourmaline commonly displays strong vertical striations running the length of its prismatic crystals. These lines are a hallmark feature that helps distinguish natural rough specimens from glass imitations and other black minerals when examining pieces by hand.

### Brazilian Supply
Brazil, and particularly the state of Minas Gerais, produces a substantial proportion of the world's gem-quality and commercial tourmaline including schorl. Much of the tumbled and raw black tourmaline available in the crystal market originates from Brazilian pegmatite deposits.

## Treatments, Trade Names, Imitations, and Care

- **Treatments:** Black Tourmaline is generally untreated.
- **Trade names:** Schorl is the mineralogical species name. Black Tourmaline is the commercial name. No widely used metaphysical trade names apply.
- **Imitations:** Black obsidian (volcanic glass, conchoidal fracture, no crystal structure), black glass, jet (organic fossil material), black kyanite (bladed habit, lower hardness in one direction), and morion (black quartz, harder, different habit) may be confused with Black Tourmaline. Natural striations on schorl crystals help identify authentic specimens.
- **Care:** Hardness 6 to 7.5; durable for most handling. Clean with mild soap and lukewarm water; avoid harsh chemicals. Avoid prolonged exposure to steam cleaning. Store away from softer stones that its rough edges or terminations could scratch.
