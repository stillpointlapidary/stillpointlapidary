## Overview

Black Tourmaline is the stone of protection, grounding, and firm boundaries. It is often chosen when surroundings feel chaotic, interpersonal limits are difficult to maintain, or attention has become scattered by sustained demands. Earth Star and Root Chakra associations reinforce the emphasis on steadiness, embodiment, and remaining anchored in one's own position.

An iron-rich member of the tourmaline group, most material sold as Black Tourmaline is schorl. It commonly forms as opaque black prismatic crystals with deep vertical striations in granitic pegmatites, granites, and metamorphic rocks. Rough crystals may be naturally terminated, broken, embedded in matrix, or polished, and visual identification becomes less certain once the original crystal habit is removed.

### Formation
Schorl forms especially in granitic pegmatites and granites, but it also occurs in metamorphic rocks and hydrothermal environments. Iron-rich chemistry produces its dark color, while trigonal growth creates the strongly striated prismatic habit familiar to collectors.

### Quality Indicators
Look for well-defined prismatic form, crisp vertical striations, intact terminations, and minimal bruising along edges. Strong form, condition, matrix association, luster, and documented locality matter more than size alone.

### Identification
Black Obsidian, manufactured glass, Jet, Morion Quartz, and Black Kyanite may cause confusion. Lengthwise striations and prismatic habit are useful clues in rough specimens, but polished material can be difficult to identify from appearance alone.

### Market & Buying Notes
Commercial Black Tourmaline is widely available in rough, tumbled, carved, and polished forms. Prices rise for sharply terminated crystals, unusual matrix specimens, strong luster, large undamaged clusters, and documented classic localities; ordinary schorl is not rare.

## Collector & Curiosity Notes

### Schorl Is the Mineral Name
- Icon: icon-book-reference

“Black Tourmaline” is the familiar market name, while schorl is the accepted mineral-species name for most material sold under it.

### Naturally Electrical
- Icon: icon-geology

Tourmaline is piezoelectric and pyroelectric, meaning pressure or temperature change can produce a measurable electrical charge; this physical property does not verify metaphysical protection claims.

### Striations Are a Useful Clue
- Icon: icon-magnifier-gem

Natural schorl commonly develops grooves parallel to the crystal's length, which can help distinguish rough crystals from smooth glass imitations.

### Abundant, but Still Collectible
- Icon: icon-rarity-tier

Schorl is the most abundant tourmaline species, but strong crystal form, intact terminations, luster, matrix association, locality, and overall condition still create collector value.

## Care & Cleaning

### Cleaning
Clean with a soft cloth or warm, mildly soapy water, then rinse and dry thoroughly.

### Water
Brief washing is appropriate for sound specimens; avoid prolonged soaking when crystals are fractured, matrix-bound, or structurally delicate.

### Light & Heat
Ordinary light is generally safe, but avoid high heat and sudden temperature changes that may damage or fracture tourmaline.

### Storage
Store separately from softer stones and protect sharp terminations from impact; rough edges can scratch more delicate materials.
