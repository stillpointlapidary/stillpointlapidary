## Overview

Selenite is the stone of spiritual clarity, used to create quiet before meditation, reflection, or intuitive work. Its bright, spacious character helps separate signal from atmosphere, making it especially useful when a room feels emotionally saturated or the mind has collected too many impressions at once. Rather than adding intensity, it establishes a threshold: pause, clear the field, and approach what follows with greater stillness. Its strongest themes are higher perspective, energetic order, and conscious connection.

A transparent crystalline habit of Gypsum, Selenite is chemically identical to fibrous Satin Spar and fine-grained Alabaster but physically distinct in form. Clear blades, tabs, and twinned crystals develop in evaporite deposits, clay beds, caves, and other sedimentary environments where calcium sulfate-rich water can crystallize. The mineral is extremely soft, readily scratched by a fingernail, and vulnerable to cleavage, moisture, and heat. Most carved white towers and wands sold as “Selenite” are actually Satin Spar Gypsum.

### Formation

Selenite forms when calcium sulfate-rich water evaporates or changes chemical conditions within sedimentary basins, clay beds, caves, and saline environments. Slow growth can produce clear blades, fishtail twins, and large transparent crystals. Its softness and water-bearing structure reflect the two water molecules held within Gypsum’s chemical formula.

### Quality Indicators

Collector-quality Selenite shows strong transparency, intact terminations, attractive twinning, minimal bruising, and a natural form suited to its locality. Large clear crystals are valued when they remain free of major cleavage damage, while unusual inclusions, phantoms, fluorescence, or dramatic twinning can add interest. For lapidary Satin Spar, even fibers, bright chatoyancy, and clean finishing matter more than transparency.

### Identification

True Selenite is the clear crystalline habit of Gypsum; Satin Spar is the parallel-fibrous habit commonly carved into white towers, bowls, and wands. Both scratch easily with a fingernail, but Satin Spar shows a silky moving sheen while Selenite is transparent to translucent and often bladed. Ulexite, Calcite, glass, and synthetic decorative materials may be confused with Gypsum, so softness, cleavage, habit, and provenance should be considered together.

### Locality Variations

Naica, Mexico, is famous for enormous transparent Gypsum crystals formed in a hot cave environment, while clay deposits in England produce clear free-floating crystals and twins. Morocco supplies abundant decorative Satin Spar, and desert environments in Mexico and the USA produce rosettes and sand-bearing Gypsum habits that should not be treated as ordinary clear Selenite.

### Physical Handling

Even a fingernail can scratch Selenite, and thin blades may cleave or splinter with surprisingly little pressure. Handle from the strongest part of the specimen, avoid rubbing fibrous edges, and never stack it loose against harder stones.

## Market & Buying Notes

Commercial naming is the central buying issue: most white carved “Selenite” is Satin Spar Gypsum. This is not fake, but it should be identified accurately. Clear crystal form, intact terminations, twinning, transparency, locality, fluorescence, and condition drive collector value, while carved Satin Spar is priced mainly by size, fiber quality, finish, and workmanship. Expect minor scratches and cleavage in soft material, but avoid undisclosed repairs, heavily bruised edges, and claims that ordinary mass-market pieces are rare.

## Care & Cleaning

### Cleaning
Dust gently with a soft dry brush or microfiber cloth and avoid scrubbing, abrasives, steam, and ultrasonic cleaning.

### Water
Avoid immersion and prolonged wetting because Gypsum is slightly water-soluble and surface damage can develop.

### Light & Heat
Normal indoor light is acceptable, but high heat can drive off structural water and leave the mineral opaque or altered.

### Storage
Store separately in a padded location where fingernails, rough cloth, and harder stones cannot scratch or cleave it.
