## Overview

Clear Quartz is the stone of amplification, clarity, and intention. Rather than carrying one narrow emotional emphasis, it is often used alone as a focal point or placed beside more specialized stones to reinforce the purpose of a practice. Its programming tradition uses the crystal as a visible anchor for the direction, quality, or ritual purpose a person chooses to reinforce.

A colorless to milky-white form of crystalline quartz, Clear Quartz is silicon dioxide that commonly develops as six-sided prisms with pyramidal terminations. Transparent material is traditionally called rock crystal. Internal veils, fractures, phantoms, fluid inclusions, mineral inclusions, and growth features may alter appearance without changing identity. Quartz may also be grown synthetically or coated for decorative effects.

### Formation
Clear Quartz grows in granitic pegmatites, hydrothermal veins, metamorphic rocks, sedimentary deposits, and open cavities where well-developed crystals can form. Natural specimens may preserve veils, phantoms, fluid inclusions, mineral inclusions, and other records of their growth environment.

### Quality Indicators
Look for intact terminations, strong surface luster, balanced crystal form, and transparency or inclusions that suit the specimen’s appeal. Chipped points, bruised edges, unstable fractures, or undisclosed repairs should reduce value.

### Identification
Glass is the most common visual imitation, while synthetic quartz and coated natural quartz raise different questions. Bubbles, mold seams, overly uniform shapes, or an unusually soft surface may suggest glass; coatings and synthetic origin should be disclosed.

### Market & Buying Notes
Clear Quartz is abundant in nearly every size and finish, from inexpensive tumbles to large clusters and collectible locality specimens. Price rises with crystal form, clarity, condition, unusual inclusions, size, documented provenance, and quality of preparation or cutting.

## Collector & Curiosity Notes

### Rock Crystal
- Icon: icon-book-reference

Transparent colorless quartz is traditionally called rock crystal, distinguishing it from milky material and colored varieties such as Amethyst, Citrine, Smoky Quartz, and Rose Quartz.

### Electrical Properties
- Icon: icon-geology

Quartz is piezoelectric and pyroelectric, properties used in technology such as frequency standards and oscillators; they do not verify metaphysical amplification claims.

### Many Crystal Forms
- Icon: icon-magnifier-gem

Quartz commonly forms six-sided prisms with pyramidal terminations, but may also be dipyramidal, tapering, needlelike, flattened, distorted, twinned, grouped, drusy, or massive.

### The Origin of “Crystal”
- Icon: icon-bookmark

The ancient Greek word *krystallos* referred to clear quartz and also carried the meaning of ice, giving the modern word *crystal* its name.

## Care & Cleaning

### Cleaning
Wash untreated pieces with mild soap, lukewarm water, and a soft cloth or brush; dry thoroughly and handle points gently.

### Water
Brief water contact is safe for untreated quartz; avoid prolonged soaking when coatings, fillers, glued components, or attached matrix are present.

### Light & Heat
Ordinary light is generally safe; avoid sudden temperature changes, sustained high heat, and conditions that could fracture the stone or damage coatings.

### Storage
Store separately or wrap points and edges; quartz can scratch softer stones and can itself be abraded by harder materials.
