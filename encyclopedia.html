How to Work With Crystals
From intention to practice
Setting Intention
Intention is what separates a stone on a shelf from a stone in practice. Before you work with a crystal, take a moment — hold it in both hands, breathe, and state what you're bringing to it. This doesn't need to be elaborate. "I'm working on letting go of anxiety" is enough. You're not casting a spell; you're directing your attention and giving the stone a context to work within.
The honest version: Crystals don't think. What intention-setting does is prime your own mind to notice relevant feelings, patterns, and shifts. The stone becomes a physical anchor for something you're already doing internally. That's not a small thing.
Ways to Work With Crystals
There's no single right method. These are the most common approaches. Start with what resonates and let your practice develop from there.

Trusting Your Intuition
The properties listed in an encyclopedia — including this one — are a starting point, not a prescription. If a stone is described as calming but it makes you feel restless, trust that. If you're drawn to a stone whose listed properties don't match what you think you need, pick it up anyway.
At intermediate level, your intuition is data. You've handled enough stones to know the difference between a random preference and a genuine pull. When your gut disagrees with a description, your experience with that stone wins.
When a Stone Doesn't Feel Right
Sometimes a stone that should work for you — by every property measure — just doesn't. This happens. The usual reasons:
It needs cleansing. A stone that's been sitting around or was handled by others may feel dense or flat. Cleanse it before writing it off.

Wrong timing. Some stones feel too activating when you're already overwhelmed, or too grounding when you need to move energy. Put it down and come back.

It's working, and you're resisting it. The stones that make us uncomfortable are sometimes doing exactly the right thing. Notice what's being stirred up before deciding it's not for you.
Crystal Forms
How form affects function, what each form does, and when to reach for it
A form is the way a stone appears as a collectible piece, naturally grown, cut, polished, carved, or shaped. The form you choose changes how you work with a stone, how you carry it, and how it fits into your space.
Think of form as function made visible. A tumble is easy to carry, a tower directs energy upward, a sphere radiates evenly, and a raw specimen keeps more of the stone's natural structure and texture.
Cleansing & Charging
Keeping your stones clear and energized
Quick Start
Cleanse new stones first
Use moonlight or sound if unsure
Avoid water unless the stone is known to be water-safe
Recharge monthly or after heavy use
Why It Matters
Crystals are energetically porous. They pick up the emotional residue of environments, people, and experiences, not metaphorically, but in the sense that a stone carried through a difficult week feels different from a freshly cleansed one. Cleansing resets that.
Charging amplifies and directs a stone's natural energy. Think of cleansing as clearing the slate, and charging as sharpening the pencil.
New stones always get cleansed first. You don't know how many hands have touched them, what environments they've been in, or what energy they've accumulated. Start clean.

How Often
There's no strict schedule — learn to read the energy. Stones used daily in active work need cleansing more often than display pieces. Signs a stone is ready: it feels heavy, dull, or suddenly unpleasant to hold. Stones you carry through stressful periods need attention after that period ends. A good default: cleanse monthly with the full moon, and anytime you feel a shift.

Crystal Families
Why a jasper is not just a jasper, and why families matter
Crystal families are defined by mineral structure and chemistry, not appearance. Stones that look nothing alike can share a family, while stones with the same name can belong to different ones. Families help you predict care needs, energetic tendencies, and why stones behave as they do.
Families are useful shortcuts. Once you know a family's behavior, you can better guess hardness, water safety, sunlight sensitivity, and how a new stone may feel in practice.
Why Families Share Energetic Tendencies
It's not coincidence that most Quartzes amplify, most Garnets energize, and most Calcites work with emotional flow. The mineral structure determines how a stone interacts with light, electricity, and subtle energy. Silicon dioxide (Quartz) is piezoelectric, generating charge under pressure, which may be why so many Quartzes feel activating. Understanding the family gives you a head start on understanding any new stone within it.
Practical implication: If you receive an unfamiliar stone and it's in the Quartz family, you already know it will likely be durable (7+ Mohs), work well with amplification, and respond well to moonlight cleansing. The family is a shortcut.

How to pick the right stone, and how to keep it

At intermediate level, trust the pull over the book.
If your gut says Black Tourmaline even though you "should" want Rose Quartz, your energy body often knows what it needs before your mind does.
Building Your Collection Over Time
The collections that serve people best aren't the biggest — they're the most intentional. Some principles that hold up over time:
Depth over breadth. Three Amethysts you've worked with deeply serve you better than thirty stones you've never connected with.

Fill the gaps deliberately. Check your collection against the major energetic role categories. If you have nothing for grounding or protection, you have a real gap.

Know what you display vs. what you work with. Display stones can be anything beautiful. Working stones should be ones you've cleansed, set intention with, and actually used.

One really good piece beats ten mediocre ones. A high-quality, ethically sourced stone has more presence than a bucket of tumbles.

The Three Main Hazards
Most crystal damage comes from three sources:
Water — Dissolves salt-based stones (Halite), rusts iron-rich ones (Pyrite, Galena), and breaks down soft stones over time. See the full water-sensitive list in the Cleansing section.
Sunlight — Fades color in UV-sensitive stones. Amethyst, Rose Quartz, Fluorite, and Celestite are the most common casualties. A sunny windowsill is a slow bleach.
Dropping — Obvious but often ignored. Calcites, Selenite, and Fluorite have perfect cleavage — they split cleanly along crystal planes. One drop on a hard floor can split a piece. Store them padded.

A small number of stones in this encyclopedia contain toxic minerals. This matters if you make crystal water (don't, unless you use the indirect method), handle raw specimens frequently, or work with children around your collection.

Crystal Grids
Using sacred geometry to amplify intention

What a grid actually does

A crystal grid is a geometric arrangement of stones set with a shared intention. The geometry matters because different shapes direct energy differently. A triangle focuses and points. A circle contains and protects. A star both sends and receives. The stones amplify each other's qualities because they're in relationship, not working in isolation.

The most important element is not the geometry. It is the moment of activation. Before you place the final stone, hold the intention clearly. State it aloud or silently. The grid holds that frequency until you dismantle it.

ActivationPlace stones from outside in, ending at the center. State your intention, then touch each stone lightly with a clear quartz point to "connect" them.
DurationMost grids are set for a specific purpose and dismantled when it resolves, whether that is a week, a month, or a season. Cleanse all stones afterward.
PlacementGrids can sit on a cloth, a mirror, sacred geometry paper, or bare wood. The surface matters less than the intention.

Mohs hardness

The Mohs scale rates a mineral's scratch resistance from 1 (softest) to 10 (hardest). It tells you how to care for a stone and whether it can safely go in water.

RatingWhat it meansExamples
1–2Very soft — scratched by a fingernail. Keep dry.Selenite, Amber, Talc
3–4Soft — scratched by a copper coin. Avoid water.Calcite, Fluorite, Azurite
5–6Medium — scratched by a knife blade.Apatite, Labradorite, Opal
6.5–7Hard — not scratched by everyday objects.Quartz, Jasper, Garnet

General water safety: stones above 6 Mohs are usually safe for brief water contact. Below 5, keep dry. Always check for toxic minerals first (e.g. Malachite, Cinnabar).

Mineral family

Stones are grouped by their mineral chemistry — what they're made of at a molecular level. Most families share physical traits like hardness range, crystal structure, and care requirements.

A few examples: all Quartzes (Amethyst, Rose Quartz, Citrine) share the same silicon dioxide base. All Feldspars (Moonstone, Labradorite, Amazonite) share a framework silicate structure. Family is the most reliable way to predict how a stone behaves physically.

How to use Still Point Lapidary

Encyclopedia

Your reference library for over 363 crystals and minerals. Search by name or use the filters — Family, Theme, Color, Chakra, Formation, and more. Click any stone to see its full profile: geology, energetic properties, chakra associations, affirmation, and reference photos. A gold dot on a card means you own that stone; an outlined ring means it's on your wishlist.

Choose by Intention

Not sure what you need? Start here. Type a feeling or intention in the AI search, or choose from the intention tiles below. Each result shows you why that stone fits your moment.

My Collection

Your personal catalog of physical pieces. Sign in to save your collection to the cloud — accessible from any device. Add pieces individually or in batches. Each entry tracks the stone type, form, location, notes, and photos.

Wishlist

Save stones you want to remember or acquire. Works alongside your collection — sign in to keep it synced across devices.

Identify

Describe a stone's color, transparency, and properties to get a shortlist of likely matches from the encyclopedia.

Crystals 101

New to crystals? Start here for foundational knowledge on how stones work, what the properties mean, and how to use them.

Your data

Sign in with a free account and your collection and wishlist are saved securely to the cloud — no backups needed, no data lost when you clear your browser.
