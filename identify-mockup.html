Identify Tab — Three Directions

Three distinct approaches to the Identify tab. All three fix the current dead-zone problem and make the progressive reveal feel intentional.

Gray boxes with colored circles represent photos Christie will take — each has a caption describing the shot.

Option A Photo Hero

A wide banner photo anchors the page — gives it visual weight, sets the editorial tone, and communicates "this is a tool for identifying real stones." The headline and subhead live inside the photo overlay (dark gradient). Below it: the card with progressive reveal steps, Tips moved inline at bottom of card.

📷 Photo needed: flat lay of 8–12 diverse stones on a white or natural linen surface — spread loosely, showing variety in color and texture. Wide crop, landscape orientation. See full photo brief at the bottom.

Option C Editorial Split

Two columns: filter steps on the left, a tall editorial photo on the right. The photo isn't decorative — it shows hands examining a stone, grounding the UI in a real-world action. On mobile, photo collapses to a smaller banner. This is the most distinctive direction — feels like a magazine tool, not a database filter.

📷 Photo needed: portrait/vertical — a pair of hands holding a crystal up to window light, stone slightly backlit so transparency is visible. Warm natural light. Clean background. See full photo brief at the bottom.

Photo Brief What to shoot
Shot A · For Option A Hero
The Collection Spread
  • 📐 Orientation: Landscape (wide)
  • 📏 Crop: Wide, shallow — maybe 3:1 ratio
  • 🪨 Stones: 8–12 pieces, variety of colors — include red, purple, green, blue, white, black at minimum
  • 🧺 Surface: White marble, raw linen, or the cream stone surface you already use
  • 💡 Light: Soft natural window light, no harsh shadows
  • 📍 Arrangement: Loose, organic — not perfectly lined up. Some stones touching, some breathing room.
  • 🚫 Avoid: Jewelry, props, text, labels, hands
This is the same kind of shot as your encyclopedia detail photos but wider — about the collection as a whole, not any single stone.
Shot B · For Option C Split
Hands & Window Light
  • 📐 Orientation: Portrait (tall)
  • 📏 Crop: Roughly square or 2:3
  • 🪨 Stone: Something translucent — Rose Quartz, Amethyst, or Calcite. One stone.
  • ✋ Hands: Hold the stone up toward a window. Fingers relaxed, not posed stiffly.
  • 💡 Light: Stone should be backlit — light coming through it shows the translucency
  • 🎨 Background: Window or bright sky out of focus — that blur of light is the whole point
  • 🚫 Avoid: Jewelry, rings, nail polish, busy background
This is the "hold it up to a window" tip made visual. The photo teaches while it decorates.
