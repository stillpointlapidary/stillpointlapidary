Overview

Citrine is the yellow to amber variety of crystalline quartz, composed of silicon dioxide and colored by iron-related color centers. Natural citrine is usually pale and relatively uncommon, while much of the deeper golden and orange material in the market is produced by heating amethyst. Both are quartz, but their color origin, zoning, and typical appearance can differ. Citrine forms in igneous, metamorphic, and hydrothermal environments, and transparent material may show internal veils, fractures, or color zoning that help distinguish one specimen from another.

Its traditional identity centers on abundance, confidence, motivation, and self-expression. Citrine is associated with Solar Plexus energy and the outward movement of personal will, linking optimism to action rather than passive wishing. Its Merchant's Stone reputation reflects its long use in prosperity and business-oriented crystal traditions. Still Point frames manifestation here as directed effort supported by confidence and clarity, not as a guarantee of material results. Heat-treated material remains quartz and carries the same public identity, though treatment should be disclosed honestly in collector and sales contexts.

Formation

Citrine is a macrocrystalline variety of quartz colored by iron-related color centers. It occurs naturally in igneous and metamorphic rocks and in hydrothermal veins. Most commercial material is produced by heating amethyst, which shifts its color from purple to yellow-orange. Deep orange-red material sold as Madeira Citrine is usually heat-treated and valued for saturation rather than natural color origin.

Common Localities

Citrine is stable under ordinary conditions, but prolonged intense light may fade some material and sustained heat can alter color. Clean with mild soap, lukewarm water, and a soft cloth. Avoid steam, harsh chemicals, sudden temperature changes, and sharp impacts that could chip crystal points or edges.

Collector & Curiosity Notes

The Merchant's Stone

Citrine's enduring association with commerce and prosperity earned it the popular name Merchant's Stone. The label belongs to modern crystal tradition rather than mineralogy, but it captures the stone's long-standing use in shops, offices, financial rituals, and goal-oriented practices.

Most Commercial Citrine Is Treated

Natural pale yellow citrine is relatively uncommon. Much of the gold and orange material sold commercially begins as amethyst and is heated to alter its color centers. The treatment is stable and widely accepted, but responsible sellers should disclose it.

Madeira Citrine

Madeira Citrine is a trade name for deeply saturated orange to reddish-orange quartz, usually produced by heating dark amethyst. The name refers to the color of Madeira wine, not to a geographic source, and finer saturated examples often command higher prices.

Natural and Heated Material

Natural and heat-treated citrine are both quartz, but they reach their color through different processes. Natural pieces are often paler and more evenly zoned, while heated amethyst may show stronger orange tones and concentrated color near crystal tips.
