Overview

Selenite is a transparent crystalline form of gypsum. Most polished wands, towers, and plates sold as selenite are actually satin spar, the fibrous, silky form of the same mineral. The retail name is broad, but the distinction matters.

Its traditions center on purification, peaceful clarity, and Crown Chakra awareness. Selenite is commonly used before meditation, around other stones, or in rooms where a lighter and more settled atmosphere is desired.

Formation

Gypsum forms in evaporite deposits, hydrothermal veins, caves, and clay-rich settings. Transparent selenite and fibrous satin spar share the same chemistry but differ in crystal habit and optical texture.

Common Localities

  • Mexico (primary source for large clear selenite)
  • Morocco
  • Algeria
  • Brazil
  • Australia
  • Poland
  • United States
  • United Kingdom

Gypsum is Mohs 2, water-sensitive, and slightly soluble. Do not soak it. Clean with a dry or barely damp soft cloth and store it separately from harder minerals.

Collector & Curiosity Notes

Selenite or Satin Spar?

Transparent sheet-like crystals are true selenite. Most familiar white wands and towers are satin spar, a fibrous gypsum form sold under the same retail name.

Named for the Moon

The name comes from Selene, the Greek moon figure, reflecting the mineral’s pale glow and translucent appearance.

The Naica Giants

Mexico’s Naica caves contain extraordinary gypsum crystals reaching monumental sizes, formed in hot mineral-rich water over immense spans of time.

Handle Gently

Gypsum is only Mohs 2 and is water-sensitive. Keep it dry, avoid soaking, and store it away from harder stones that can scratch it.
