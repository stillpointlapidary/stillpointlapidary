Mohs Hardness Is Not the Whole Story

What Scratch Resistance Can Tell You, and What It Cannot

About 5 minutes

You look up a stone and see Mohs 7. That sounds reassuring. Seven is fairly high, so the stone must be sturdy.

A high Mohs number can help explain why one stone keeps a smooth polish while another picks up scratches. What it cannot tell you is whether a crystal point will chip, how well a stone handles impact, or whether heat, light, water, or chemicals can affect it.

Mohs measures scratch resistance, not overall strength.

But sturdy in what way?

The job of the number

What the Number Actually Tells You

Quartz makes the distinction easy to see. Quartz has a Mohs hardness of 7. Fluorite is 4. Quartz can scratch fluorite because it ranks higher on the scale.

That is exactly what Mohs was built to tell you.

Mohs answers one precise question: how resistant is this material to scratching?

Primary teaching visual

The Mohs Scale at a Glance

The scale uses ten reference minerals. The numbers give you the order, but the gaps between them are not equal.

A ranking, not an evenly spaced ruler.

Diamond is not simply a little harder than corundum because 10 comes after 9. The scale tells you the order. It does not tell you that each step represents the same amount of difference.

Keep the job narrow

What Mohs Helps You Understand

01 · Stone against stone

Which stone can scratch another?

Quartz can scratch fluorite, calcite, gypsum, and other softer minerals. That matters in drawers, travel pouches, and storage.

02 · Surface wear

How easily might a polish show wear?

A softer polished stone may pick up fine scratches more readily under the same kind of contact.

03 · Identification clue

Can scratching help identify a mineral?

Scratch resistance can narrow the possibilities, but it is only one clue rather than a complete identification method.

The central twist

A Higher Number Does Not Mean Stronger in Every Way

Topaz has a Mohs hardness of 8. Nephrite jade is usually around 6 to 6.5, so topaz resists scratching better.

Jade, however, is generally tougher. Its structure makes it better able to withstand breaking and chipping.

They are strong in different ways.

Neither material is simply “stronger.” Hardness and toughness answer different questions. Mohs is excellent at the one it was designed for: scratch resistance.

Choose the right question

What Are You Trying to Learn?

When you want to understand how a stone may behave, first ask what you are actually trying to learn.

Will it scratch?
Mohs hardness
This is the question the scale answers beautifully.
Will it chip or break?
Toughness and internal structure
You need different information.
Can its surroundings affect it?
Stability
Think heat, light, water, and chemicals.

This is less about memorizing three categories than knowing which question you are asking.

Same mineral, different object

The Shape of the Stone Matters Too

A quartz sphere and a quartz cluster both have a Mohs hardness of 7, but they are not equally vulnerable in practice.

The mineral is the same. The object is not.

That is why a hardness number should never replace looking at the actual specimen in front of you.

Put it to work

Why This Is Useful for Collectors

Once you know what Mohs actually measures, several everyday choices become easier to understand.

Use Mohs for the Surface

  • Keep harder stones from rubbing against softer ones.
  • Remove gritty dust gently instead of grinding it across polish.
  • Treat fine points and delicate clusters according to their shape.
  • Check heat, light, water, and cleaning guidance separately.

None of this means the Mohs scale is incomplete. It means the scale is precise. It answers one question extremely well, which makes it more useful once you stop asking it to answer the others.

The number is the beginning

Now the Number Means Something

Mohs hardness tells you who can scratch whom. Once that distinction clicks, the number stops being a vague signal of “strength” and becomes a specific piece of information you can actually use.

The number matters because now you know exactly what it means.
